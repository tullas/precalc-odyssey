Advanced trigonometry

Beyond the unit circle: shaping waves and using identities as tools.

Why go further than sin and cos of acute angles?

Once sine and cosine are coordinates on the unit circle, the same functions model anything that repeats: sound pressure, seasonal daylight, alternating current, a spinning wheel’s height. Engineers tune amplitude (how tall the wave is) and period (how long one cycle lasts). The model

y = A sin(Bx)

puts those knobs in the formula: |A| is amplitude; period is 2π/|B|.

Real signals

  • Audio — louder is larger amplitude; pitch relates to frequency (linked to B).
  • Day length over a year — roughly wave-like around a midline.
  • Ferris wheel height vs time — sinusoidal in the ideal model.
  • Heart monitors and sensors — repeating patterns analyzed with wave language.

Identities as rewrite rules

The Pythagorean identity sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 is the unit circle in algebra form. Double-angle formulas let you rewrite sin(2θ) or cos(2θ) when a model or equation needs it. Treat them as tools—like factoring—not as decoration.
