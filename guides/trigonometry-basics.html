Trigonometry basics

Angles on the unit circle, sine and cosine as coordinates, and the wave graph—with live interactive diagrams.

Trigonometry begins with angles and right triangles, then expands to the unit circle: every angle places a point (cos θ, sin θ) on a circle of radius 1. Use the interactive panels below—both the circle and the wave respond to drag, slider, and preset buttons.

Unit circle

Interactive Drag the green point Or use slider

Try it: Press and drag the glowing green point around the circle. Cosine is the horizontal dashed leg; sine is the vertical one.

What the diagram is showing

  • Green point — position on the unit circle (drag me)
  • Purple dashed — cosine (x-coordinate)
  • Green dashed — sine (y-coordinate)
  • Yellow arc — angle θ from the positive x-axis

On a circle of radius 1, the point is always (cos θ, sin θ). That is why cos²θ + sin²θ = 1 is the circle equation.

Radians

A radian measures angle by arc length on the unit circle: a full turn is 2π radians (360°). The readout shows degrees and radians together.

From circle to wave

As θ advances, the height of the green point is sin θ. The panel below plots that height against θ. You can drag the marker on the wave too—circle and wave stay linked.

Sine wave linked to the same angle

Interactive Drag the marker Same θ as circle

Try it: Drag left/right on the wave (or on the green dot). The vertical line is θ; the dot’s height is sin θ—the same value as on the circle.

Check: Set θ to 90° (preset or drag). Cosine ≈ 0, sine ≈ 1—point at the top of the circle; wave marker at its first peak.
