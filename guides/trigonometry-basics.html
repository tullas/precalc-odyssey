Trigonometry basics

Angles on the unit circle, sine and cosine as coordinates, and the wave graph.

Trigonometry begins with angles and right triangles, then expands to the unit circle: every angle places a point (cos θ, sin θ) on a circle of radius 1. That single idea powers graphs, polar coordinates, and many physics models.

Unit circle

Radians in one sentence

A radian measures angle by arc length on the unit circle: a full turn is 2π radians (360°). Precalculus and calculus prefer radians for calculus-ready formulas.

The wave

Plotting sin θ as θ runs produces the classic wave. Amplitude, period, and midline adjustments appear in models of sound, seasons, and circular motion.
