Trigonometry basics

Angles on the unit circle, sine and cosine as coordinates, and the wave graph—with a live interactive diagram.

Trigonometry begins with angles and right triangles, then expands to the unit circle: every angle places a point (cos θ, sin θ) on a circle of radius 1. Drag the point or use the slider below to see that idea in motion.

Interactive unit circle

Drag the green point around the circle, or move the slider. Cosine is the horizontal leg; sine is the vertical leg.

What the diagram is showing

  • Green point — position on the unit circle
  • Purple dashed — cosine (x-coordinate, horizontal)
  • Green dashed — sine (y-coordinate, vertical)
  • Yellow arc — the angle θ from the positive x-axis

On a circle of radius 1, the point is always (cos θ, sin θ). That is why the Pythagorean identity cos²θ + sin²θ = 1 is just the circle equation.

Radians in one sentence

A radian measures angle by arc length on the unit circle: a full turn is 2π radians (360°). Calculus prefers radians; the readout above shows both.

From circle to wave

As θ runs forward, the height of the green point is sin θ. Plot that height against θ and you get the sine wave. Move the slider and watch how the height matches the wave marker.

Sine wave linked to the same angle

The vertical line tracks the current θ. Its height is sin θ—the same number as on the circle.

Try this: Set θ to 90°. Cosine should be about 0 and sine about 1—the point is at the top. At 180°, you are at the left: cos ≈ −1, sin ≈ 0.
