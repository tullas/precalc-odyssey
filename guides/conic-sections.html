Conic sections

Parabolas, circles, ellipses, hyperbolas—and why engineers and astronomers care.

Where the name comes from

Slice a double cone with a plane and the cut edge is a conic section. In algebra the same shapes appear as second-degree equations in x and y. The geometry and the algebra describe one family.

Real uses you can point to

  • Parabola — satellite dishes and flashlight reflectors focus rays through a point (the focus). Arch shapes in some bridges approximate parabolas.
  • Ellipse — planetary orbits (Kepler): the sun at one focus. Whispering galleries use elliptical reflection.
  • Circle — special ellipse with equal axes; wheels, gears, compass curves.
  • Hyperbola — some navigation systems and cooling-tower silhouettes; inverse-proportion style branches in models.

Ellipse stretcher

Equation idea: x²/a² + y²/b² = 1. When a = b you get a circle. Changing a and b stretches the oval—useful intuition before formal focus-directrix definitions.
