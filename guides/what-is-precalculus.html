What is precalculus?

Not calculus early—function sense, graphs, and modeling so calculus can talk about change clearly.

The short answer

Precalculus is the course between intermediate algebra and calculus. Its job is to make functions, graphs, and modeling feel natural. Calculus studies how things change; precalculus makes sure you can name and graph the things that are changing.

How it differs from algebra

Algebra often asks “find x.” Precalculus still does that, but more often asks “what does this graph say?” and “how do I adjust this formula when the story shifts?” Functions become objects: you shift them, stretch them, compose them, and invert them.

Where these ideas show up in ordinary life

  • Maps and GPS — coordinates, distances, directions (related to vectors and polar thinking).
  • Interest, inflation, epidemics in the news — exponential language (percent change over time).
  • Sound, daylight hours, tides — wave-like patterns (trigonometry).
  • Speeds and averages — rates of change (the bridge toward calculus).
  • Computer graphics and games — transformations, parametric motion, matrices for scaling and rotation.

You may not write log on a grocery list, but the same structures appear whenever something multiplies, oscillates, or needs a direction—not only an amount.

Typical topics

  • Functions and transformations
  • Polynomial and rational functions
  • Exponential and logarithmic functions
  • Trigonometry
  • Often also: sequences, conics, vectors, polar, parametric, matrices, rates and limits

How to study it well

Memorizing steps without graphs is fragile. A stronger loop is: read a short explanation → play with an interactive diagram → solve a story problem → read a worked solution. That is how Hyper-Scale Lab is built: guides for ideas, lab for practice.
