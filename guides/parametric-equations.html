Parametric equations: motion through time

A path is not enough when you also need to know when something was there.

The idea

A graph y = f(x) answers “what height for this x?” It cannot easily describe a vertical segment, a loop, or going back along the same road later. Parametric equations give both coordinates as functions of a third variable—often time t:

x = x(t),   y = y(t)

One clock drives both. As t runs, the point (x(t), y(t)) traces a path—and the schedule of that motion is built in.

Where this shows up

  • Animation and games — every frame is a value of t; position is updated each tick.
  • GPS tracks — not only the road shape, but timestamps along it.
  • Ferris wheels and gears — angle or time parameter spins a point on a circle.
  • Thrown ball (physics) — horizontal and vertical positions both depend on time.
  • Security camera footage — “where at 3:02?” is parametric thinking.

Time scrubber (circular path)

Interactive Scrub t

Try it: x = 4 cos t, y = 4 sin t. Move t through a full turn and watch the point orbit. Path is the ring; t is the schedule.

x = 4 cos t = 4.00
y = 4 sin t = 0.00

Trap: Eliminating t shows the geometric path but discards timing. Two runners can share a track equation and still finish at different times.
